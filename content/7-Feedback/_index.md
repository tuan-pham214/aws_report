## General Impression

The **First Cloud AI Journey** program gave me an internship experience with a strong practical focus. Instead of studying AWS services separately, I participated in a project that covered problem analysis, architectural design, deployment, integration, testing, and result presentation. This helped me understand the difference between learning a technology and applying that technology to solve a specific problem.

During the internship, I experienced working as part of a team and proactively coordinating with related workstreams. In the Local AQI project, the backend could not be completed independently; it required alignment with the ingestion, Data, and ML members. This was a valuable experience because I learned to communicate through schemas, API contracts, and test results rather than relying only on verbal descriptions.

## Learning and Working Environment

The program provided an open learning environment that encouraged students to conduct their own research. I had opportunities to experiment on AWS, encounter real issues, and investigate their causes before discussing them with a mentor. This approach helped me retain knowledge and develop the habit of looking for evidence in logs, documentation, and resource status.

The accompanying community activities and events also gave me a broader view of careers in cloud computing. In addition to technical knowledge, the speakers discussed learning attitudes, communication, teamwork, and the responsibilities involved in operating real systems.

## Support from Mentors and the Program Team

The mentors and FCAJ team supported students by providing direction, answering questions, and monitoring progress. What I appreciated most was that mentors did not simply provide answers. Instead, they often suggested an approach that allowed students to investigate and make their own decisions. This helped me become more confident when working on unfamiliar problems.

The learning materials and workshops provided the foundation required to begin working with AWS. During the project, I was able to build on that knowledge and study EC2, IAM, S3, SageMaker, DynamoDB, SNS, and CloudWatch according to the needs of my responsibilities.

## Relevance to My Major and Career Direction

The internship content was relevant to my **Computer Science** major and my interest in Backend and Cloud Engineering. The project required knowledge of programming, APIs, databases, computer networks, security, and system deployment. Integrating the forecast model also helped me understand how a backend communicates with a machine learning component in a practical product.

After the program, I became more certain that I would like to continue developing as a **Backend Engineer** while expanding my knowledge of cloud infrastructure and system operations.

## What I Valued Most

What I valued most was that the team completed a product flow that could be demonstrated directly: telemetry data was sent through IoT Core and Firehose to S3, processed for forecasting, returned to FastAPI through a SageMaker Endpoint, and combined with DynamoDB and SNS to support subscriptions and AQI email alerts.

This process allowed me to see how my individual work contributed to a larger system. Having the API deployed on EC2 and accessible through Swagger also made the project feel less like a programming exercise and more like a product that could be deployed and tested.

## Suggestions for the Program

- Organize an architecture and data-contract alignment session at the beginning of the project stage so that teams can reduce integration rework.
- Add short technical reviews at major milestones, focusing on architecture, security, cost, and testability instead of reviewing only general progress.
- Provide the demo and handover checklist earlier so students can prepare logs, evidence images, test accounts, and fallback plans when services respond slowly.
- If possible, include a code review or simulated incident-response session to help students understand production expectations.
- Continue organizing community events so students can meet engineers working in the industry and gain a clearer understanding of employer expectations.

## Personal Expectations

I would like to continue participating in FCAJ community activities and more advanced programs related to Backend Engineering, cloud architecture, and AI applications. I would recommend this program to friends interested in cloud computing because it provides not only AWS knowledge but also opportunities to complete a team project, connect with the community, and practice presenting a technical product.

Overall, the internship was a meaningful experience for me. The program helped me identify areas where my knowledge is still limited and motivated me to continue learning and building higher-quality projects in the future.
